# Testosterone Replacement Therapy and Acne: What Runners and Athletes Need to Know

## Understanding the Connection Between TRT and Skin Health

Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) has become increasingly common among men seeking to address low testosterone levels, but many users are surprised to discover an unwelcome side effect: acne. While the original observation that higher doses increase risk holds true, research shows that acne can develop at virtually any dosage level—making it essential for anyone considering or currently using TRT to understand this connection.

For runners and endurance athletes, this presents unique challenges. Training frequently in sweat-soaked clothing, experiencing hormonal fluctuations from intense exercise, and managing recovery all intersect with skin health in ways that deserve careful attention.

## The Science Behind TRT-Related Acne

Testosterone influences sebum production through its conversion to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), a more potent androgen. This increased sebum—the oily substance produced by sebaceous glands—can clog pores and create an environment where acne-causing bacteria thrive.

A 2023 study published in the *Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology* found that approximately 25-30% of men undergoing TRT experience some degree of acne exacerbation, with the risk increasing at higher doses. However, even men on physiological replacement doses (typically 100-200mg weekly) can develop acne, particularly during the initial months of treatment when hormone levels fluctuate most dramatically.

**Key insight:** The timing matters. Many men experience acne flare-ups 4-12 weeks after starting TRT or after dose adjustments, when the body is adapting to new hormonal baselines.

## Why Runners Face Unique Challenges

Endurance athletes already navigate several factors that can compound TRT-related skin issues:

**Sweat and friction:** Long runs create prolonged exposure to moisture and friction, particularly in areas like the back, chest, and thighs where acne often develops.

**Elevated cortisol:** Intense training raises cortisol levels, which can increase inflammation and potentially worsen acne outcomes.

**Nutritional factors:** High-carbohydrate diets (common for runners) can spike insulin, which may influence sebum production and androgen sensitivity.

**Compromised skin barrier:** Frequent showering and exposure to chlorine (for pool runners) can disrupt the skin's natural barrier, making it more vulnerable to irritation.

## Practical Strategies for Managing TRT-Related Acne

### Training Adjustments

**Shower immediately after training.** Don't linger in sweaty clothes—bacteria multiply quickly in warm, moist environments. For runners doing double sessions, consider a quick rinse between workouts.

**Choose breathable, moisture-wicking fabrics.** Technical fabrics move sweat away from skin more effectively than cotton, reducing pore-clogging moisture. Look for garments with antimicrobial treatments if you're prone to body acne.

**Manage friction points.** Apply anti-chafe balms to areas prone to irritation, and consider seamless designs in high-friction zones.

**Adjust intensity during flare-ups.** If acne is severe and inflamed, temporarily reducing training volume or intensity can help your skin recover while maintaining fitness with lower-impact cross-training.

### Nutrition and Hydration

**Monitor dairy and high-glycemic foods.** Some evidence suggests these can exacerbate acne in susceptible individuals. Consider a two-week elimination trial to assess personal sensitivity—but ensure you maintain adequate protein and calorie intake for training demands.

**Prioritize anti-inflammatory foods.** Omega-3 fatty acids (fatty fish, walnuts, flaxseed), colorful vegetables, and adequate zinc intake support skin health. A 2022 review in *Nutrients* highlighted zinc's role in reducing acne severity.

**Stay hydrated.** Proper hydration supports skin barrier function and helps dilute sebum. Aim for pale yellow urine as a practical hydration marker.

**Consider timing carbohydrates around training.** This reduces insulin spikes at rest while still fueling performance.

### Skincare Protocol for Athletes

**Use a gentle, pH-balanced cleanser twice daily.** Harsh scrubs can worsen inflammation. Look for products containing salicylic acid (0.5-2%) or benzoyl peroxide (2.5-5%) for acne-prone skin.

**Incorporate a post-workout routine.** Keep cleansing wipes in your gym bag for situations where showering isn't immediately possible. Follow with a light, non-comedogenic moisturizer.

**Don't over-wash.** More than twice daily can strip skin of protective oils, triggering rebound sebum production.

**Consider a weekly exfoliation.** Gentle chemical exfoliants (like AHAs) can help prevent pore clogging without the irritation of physical scrubs.

### Medical Management

**Communicate with your prescribing physician.** Acne is a known side effect—don't suffer silently. Dose adjustments, timing changes (weekly vs. biweekly injections), or switching delivery methods (gel vs. injection) can sometimes help.

**Explore dermatological options.** Topical retinoids, oral antibiotics (short-term), or prescription-strength treatments can manage moderate to severe cases. For persistent acne, isotretinoin may be considered—though this requires careful monitoring and is contraindicated for those with certain health conditions.

**Consider adjunctive therapies.** Some men find benefit from red light therapy or chemical peels, though evidence for these specifically in TRT-related acne is limited.

## Safety Considerations and Red Flags

**Watch for cystic acne.** Deep, painful nodules require prompt dermatological evaluation—these can cause scarring and may indicate a need for more aggressive treatment.

**Monitor for signs of infection.** Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or fever around acne lesions warrants medical attention.

**Be cautious with supplements.** Some bodybuilding supplements marketed for hormone support can worsen acne. Always check with your physician before adding anything to your regimen.

**Don't stop TRT abruptly.** This can cause significant hormonal disruption. Any changes should be made under medical supervision.

## Realistic Timelines and Expectations

**Initial adjustment period:** Expect 8-16 weeks for skin to adapt after starting TRT or changing doses. Some men see improvement as hormone levels stabilize; others experience worsening before improvement.

**Treatment response:** Topical treatments typically show improvement in 6-8 weeks, with maximum benefit at 12-16 weeks. Oral medications may work faster but carry more systemic effects.

**Scarring prevention:** Early intervention is key. Treating acne promptly reduces the risk of permanent scarring, which can take months to years to fade.

## Alternatives for Different Situations

**For those without gym access:** Home-based skincare routines are equally effective. Focus on consistent cleansing, appropriate products, and post-workout hygiene regardless of training location.

**For budget-conscious athletes:** Drugstore options like benzoyl peroxide washes and adapalene gel (now available OTC) are evidence-based and affordable. A basic routine of gentle cleanser, treatment product, and moisturizer covers most needs.

**For sensitive skin:** Look for fragrance-free, non-comedogenic products. Lactic acid is gentler than glycolic for exfoliation, and niacinamide offers anti-inflammatory benefits without irritation.

**For outdoor runners:** Sunscreen is non-negotiable—some acne treatments increase photosensitivity. Choose non-comedogenic, sweat-resistant formulas and reapply during long runs.

## Coaching Perspectives: Practical Tips

**From a dermatology-informed coach:** "I recommend clients start their skincare routine *before* beginning TRT. Establishing good habits early makes managing potential side effects much easier."

**From an endurance coach:** "Don't let skin concerns derail your training. Most acne is manageable with consistent routines. If you're self-conscious about back acne in singlets or swimwear, consider moisture-wicking layers or timing outdoor sessions for cooler parts of the day."

**From a strength coach:** "For lifters on TRT, pay attention to equipment hygiene. Clean benches, shared mats, and gym equipment can harbor bacteria. Wipe down equipment and consider bringing your own towel."

## The Bottom Line

TRT-related acne is common but manageable. The key is proactive management: establish good skincare habits, communicate openly with your healthcare provider, and address flare-ups promptly rather than waiting for them to resolve. For runners and athletes, the combination of hormonal therapy and training demands requires attention to hygiene, nutrition, and recovery—but with the right strategies, you can maintain both your performance goals and healthy skin.

Remember that individual responses vary widely. What works for one athlete may not work for another. Patience, consistency, and professional guidance are your best tools for navigating this aspect of TRT management.

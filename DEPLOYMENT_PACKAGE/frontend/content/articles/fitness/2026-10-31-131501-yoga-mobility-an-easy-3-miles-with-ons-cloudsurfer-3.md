# An Easy 3 Miles with On's Cloudsurfer 3: A Runner's Honest Review

## A City Jog That Changed a Decade of Shoe Loyalty

After years of stubborn loyalty to another brand, could one city jog make a longtime runner reconsider his shoes? That's the question I set out to answer on a relaxed three-mile loop through the city—and the answer surprised me more than I expected.

As runners, we tend to become creatures of habit. We find a shoe that works, buy it in bulk, and defend it like a favorite sports team. But exercise science keeps evolving, and so does footwear technology. According to industry data, the global running shoe market continues to grow at roughly 5–6% annually, driven largely by innovations in midsole foams and rocker geometry—the very features that define On's Cloudsurfer line. Staying curious, it turns out, is part of staying healthy.

## Why a "Simple 3 Miles" Matters More Than You Think

Before diving into the shoe, let's talk about the run itself. An easy three-miler is one of the most underrated workouts in any training plan—and one of the most important.

**The science of easy running:** Research consistently shows that roughly 80% of weekly mileage should be performed at low intensity (Zone 1–2, or a conversational pace). This polarized approach improves aerobic capacity, capillarization, and mitochondrial density while keeping injury risk manageable. A 2021 review in *Frontiers in Physiology* reinforced that easy running builds the aerobic base that supports harder efforts later.

**Practical coaching tip:** If you can't speak in full sentences during your "easy" run, slow down. Most runners run their easy days too fast—a mistake that blunts recovery and increases overuse injury risk.

## First Impressions: On's Cloudsurfer 3 on the Road

### The Feel Underfoot

The Cloudsurfer 3 features On's updated CloudTec Phase midsole—a series of interconnected "cloud" pods designed to collapse sequentially for smoother transitions. On my three-mile loop, the ride felt notably soft without being mushy. The rocker geometry encouraged a gentle forward roll, which many runners find reduces ankle and calf strain on longer efforts.

### Who This Shoe Suits

- **Beginners:** The forgiving cushioning and smooth roll make this a friendly daily trainer for new runners building a base. Expect to adapt over 2–4 weeks as your feet and lower legs adjust to any new shoe geometry.
- **Intermediate runners:** A solid choice for recovery days and easy mileage—the 80% of your training that shouldn't feel hard.
- **Advanced runners:** Best used as a recovery-day or easy-run companion rather than a speed shoe. Pair it with a firmer trainer or race-day shoe for workouts.

### Alternatives by Budget and Environment

- **Budget option:** Look for previous-generation models of any daily trainer—often 30–40% cheaper with minimal performance difference.
- **Treadmill runners:** Cushioned, rockered shoes work well indoors; just be aware treadmill belts can exaggerate wear on outsole rubber.
- **Trail access:** Keep this shoe on pavement. For mixed terrain, choose a shoe with deeper lugs and a rock plate.

## Form Cues for an Easy Three-Miler

Proper form matters regardless of footwear. Coach-recommended cues:

1. **Run tall:** Imagine a string lifting the crown of your head.
2. **Cadence over stride:** Aim for a quicker, lighter turnover (roughly 170–180 steps per minute is a common benchmark, though individual variation is normal).
3. **Land under your hips:** Avoid overstriding, which increases braking forces and knee load.
4. **Relaxed shoulders and hands:** Tension travels—keep your jaw and grip loose.

## Nutrition and Recovery for Short Easy Runs

Even a three-miler benefits from smart fueling:

- **Pre-run:** A small snack 60–90 minutes out (banana, toast with honey) if you're running in the morning.
- **Hydration:** 400–600 ml of water in the 2 hours before running is a reasonable guideline for most conditions; increase in heat.
- **Post-run:** A snack with carbs and protein within 60–90 minutes supports recovery—Greek yogurt with fruit or a handful of nuts and a banana works well.
- **Everyday protein:** General sports nutrition guidance suggests 1.4–1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight daily for recreational runners supporting muscle repair.

## Injury Prevention and Realistic Expectations

New shoes won't fix training errors—but they can support good habits:

- **Rotate shoes:** Alternating between two pairs can reduce repetitive loading patterns and extend shoe lifespan. Most running shoes last 500–800 km depending on body weight and surface.
- **Progress gradually:** Follow the 10% rule as a rough guide when increasing weekly mileage.
- **Strength work:** Two short sessions weekly—focusing on calves, glutes, and hip stability—meaningfully reduce injury risk. Research suggests strength training can cut running injury rates by up to half.
- **Listen to your body:** Persistent pain lasting more than a few days warrants rest and, if needed, professional assessment.

**Timeline reality check:** Expect 3–4 weeks to adapt to a new shoe and 6–8 weeks of consistent easy running before meaningful aerobic gains show up. Patience is a training tool.

## The Verdict

Could one city jog make a longtime runner reconsider his shoes? For me—yes, with nuance. The Cloudsurfer 3 excels as an easy-day trainer: comfortable, smooth, and beginner-friendly. It won't replace a dedicated speed shoe, but it earned a spot in the rotation. The real lesson isn't about brand loyalty at all—it's about staying curious, training sensibly, and letting evidence and feel guide your choices.

**Actionable takeaway:** Give any new shoe a fair three-run trial on easy terrain, keep your easy days genuinely easy, and invest as much in recovery and strength work as you do in gear. Your miles—and your joints—will thank you.

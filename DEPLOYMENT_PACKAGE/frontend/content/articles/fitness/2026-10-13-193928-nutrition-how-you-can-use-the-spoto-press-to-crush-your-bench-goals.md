# How You Can Use the Spoto Press to Crush Your Bench Goals

*This variation will power up your bench to new heights — and the science backs it up.*

If your bench press has been stuck at the same number for months, you're not alone. Plateaus are the rule, not the exception, especially once you're moving serious weight. But before you blame your programming, your genetics, or your pre-workout, consider this: the problem might not be how hard you're pressing. It might be *where* you're pressing.

Enter the Spoto press — a deceptively simple bench variation named after world-record powerlifter Eric Spoto, who famously pressed 722 pounds raw. By pausing just above your chest instead of touching it, you strip away momentum, expose weak points, and force your muscles to do the honest work. It's one of the most effective tools for building a bigger, more resilient bench press, and it requires nothing more than a barbell, a bench, and a willingness to check your ego at the rack.

## What Exactly Is a Spoto Press?

A Spoto press is a bench press performed with a deliberate pause one to two inches above your chest — never touching — before pressing the bar back up. That's it. No chains, no bands, no special equipment.

The magic lies in what the pause removes: the stretch reflex. When you bounce a bar off your chest, your pecs, shoulders, and triceps store elastic energy during the descent and release it on the way up. That bounce can add meaningful pounds to your lift — and it can also mask weaknesses, irritate your shoulders, and teach sloppy bar path habits.

By hovering above the chest, you eliminate the bounce entirely. Every rep becomes a pure test of starting strength and control from your most vulnerable position. Coaches have long used paused variations for this reason, and the Spoto press has become a staple in powerlifting and general strength programs alike.

## Why the Spoto Press Works: The Evidence

The rationale for paused pressing is well-established in strength science. Research on bench press variations consistently shows that adding a pause at the chest increases muscle activation and time under tension while reducing the contribution of the stretch-shortening cycle. A widely cited study from the Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research found that paused bench presses significantly reduced velocity and required greater force production during the ascent compared to touch-and-go reps — exactly the stimulus you want for building strength out of the bottom position.

Electromyographic (EMG) analyses have also shown high activation of the pectoralis major, anterior deltoid, and triceps during paused pressing, particularly in the mid-to-lower range of motion. In other words, the Spoto press trains the exact muscles that fail first when a heavy bench stalls.

There's a stability dividend, too. Holding the bar motionless above your chest demands trunk and scapular control, which carries over to every other press you perform.

## The Benefits Beyond a Bigger Bench

**1. It fixes your sticking point.** Most lifters fail a few inches off the chest. The Spoto press lives in that zone.

**2. It builds shoulder-friendly strength.** Eliminating the bounce reduces the abrupt shear forces at the shoulder joint. Many lifters with cranky shoulders find paused work more comfortable than touch-and-go pressing.

**3. It sharpens bar path and technique.** You can't muscle through a Spoto press with sloppy form. The pause forces you to stay tight, keep your wrists stacked, and drive through your legs.

**4. It transfers to the competition lift.** Powerlifters have used paused bench work for decades specifically because it improves the raw bench.

## How to Perform the Spoto Press: Step-by-Step

**Setup:**
- Lie on a flat bench with your eyes directly under the bar.
- Plant your feet firmly, roughly shoulder-width, and drive them into the floor.
- Retract and depress your shoulder blades — imagine squeezing a pencil between them and pinning them to the bench.
- Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width, wrists stacked over your elbows.
- Unrack with straight arms, then lock your elbows and pull the bar into position over your lower chest.

**The Descent:**
- Lower the bar under control, taking about two seconds. Keep your elbows at roughly a 45-to-70-degree angle from your torso — not flared to 90 degrees.
- Stop one to two inches above your chest. A good cue: "hover where a fist could fit between the bar and your shirt."

**The Pause:**
- Hold for one to three seconds. Stay tight — don't relax into the pause. Your body should feel coiled, not collapsed.

**The Press:**
- Drive the bar up and slightly back toward your face, keeping your shoulder blades pinned.
- Lock out, then repeat. Reset your breath and brace on every rep.

## Programming the Spoto Press for Your Level

**Beginner (less than six months of consistent training):**
- Use the Spoto press as a technique builder, not a max-strength lift.
- 3 sets of 5 to 6 reps at 55–65% of your regular bench 1RM, with a 2-second pause.
- Once a week is plenty. Keep your standard bench press as the main lift.

**Intermediate (6 months to 2 years):**
- 4 sets of 4 to 5 reps at 70–80% of your bench 1RM, 2-second pause.
- Use it as a secondary movement after your main bench, or alternate weeks between Spoto and touch-and-go pressing.
- Add 2.5 to 5 pounds per week when you can complete all sets with clean form.

**Advanced (2+ years, competitive or near-competitive):**
- 5 sets of 3 reps at 80–85% of your 1RM with a 2-to-3-second pause, or work up to a heavy single or triple.
- Consider a "Spoto wave": Week 1 at 75%, Week 2 at 80%, Week 3 at 85%, Week 4 deload.
- Pair with close-grip bench and overhead press for balanced development.

**Sample 4-week block (intermediate):**
- Week 1: 4x5 @ 70%
- Week 2: 4x4 @ 75%
- Week 3: 4x4 @ 78%
- Week 4: 3x3 @ 82%, then reassess

## Realistic Timelines: What to Expect

- **Weeks 1–3:** You'll likely need to reduce weight by 10–20% versus your touch-and-go bench. That's normal and expected.
- **Weeks 4–8:** Your bottom-position strength improves noticeably. Many lifters find their regular bench feels lighter off the chest.
- **Weeks 8–12:** Expect a 5–15 pound increase on your touch-and-go bench, depending on training age and consistency. Advanced lifters should expect smaller jumps — 5 to 10 pounds is a solid win.
- **Beyond 12 weeks:** Continued progress requires periodization, adequate recovery, and attention to your overall pressing volume.

No single exercise adds 50 pounds to your bench in a month. Anyone promising that is selling something. The Spoto press is a powerful tool, not a magic trick.

## Nutrition and Recovery: The Other Half of the Equation

You can't out-train poor recovery. To actually convert Spoto press work into a bigger bench, support it with the basics:

**Protein:** Aim for 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight daily (roughly 0.7–1 gram per pound). Research consistently supports this range for maximizing muscle protein synthesis and strength gains. Spread it across three to five meals.

**Calories:** If your goal is strength, eat at maintenance or a slight surplus (200–300 calories above maintenance). Aggressive cutting and max-strength gains rarely coexist.

**Carbohydrates:** Don't fear them. Carbs fuel high-intensity pressing. Target 3 to 5 grams per kilogram of bodyweight on training days for most lifters.

**Hydration:** Even mild dehydration (1–2% of bodyweight) can impair strength performance. Drink 16 to 20 ounces of water in the two hours before training.

**Sleep:** Seven to nine hours per night. Sleep deprivation measurably reduces force production and slows recovery. This is non-negotiable if you're chasing PRs.

**Timing:** A meal or snack with 20 to 40 grams of protein and 30 to 60 grams of carbs one to two hours pre-training supports performance. Post-training, hit another protein dose within a few hours — the "anabolic window" is wider than once believed, but consistent daily intake matters most.

## Safety Considerations and Form Cues

- **Don't go too heavy too soon.** The Spoto press is humbling. Start lighter than you think you need.
- **Use a spotter or safety pins.** Always. A failed rep two inches off your chest is a dangerous place to be.
- **Keep your wrists neutral.** A bent-back wrist under load invites injury.
- **Don't flare your elbows.** Tucking to 45–70 degrees protects the shoulder joint.
- **Stay braced.** Big breath into the belly, ribs down, glutes engaged.
- **Stop if you feel shoulder or elbow pain.** Discomfort in the muscles is fine; joint pain is a red flag.

## Alternatives and Equipment Options

- **No barbell?** Use dumbbells and pause an inch above your chest. The stability demand is higher, so reduce the load.
- **Smith machine:** A solid option for learning the pause pattern safely.
- **Floor press:** If your shoulders are sensitive, the floor press limits range of motion and pairs well with Spoto-style pauses.
- **Pin press:** Set pins just above your chest and press from a dead stop for an even more extreme starting-strength challenge.
- **Band-resisted Spoto press:** Add accommodating resistance for advanced lifters seeking a new stimulus.

## Coaching Tips That Make a Difference

1. **Video your sets from the side.** Most lifters pause higher than they think. Verify the bar is truly one to two inches off the chest.
2. **Count the pause out loud or with a metronome.** Consistency beats guesswork.
3. **Prioritize quality over load.** Three crisp sets beat five sloppy ones every time.
4. **Rotate it in and out.** Use the Spoto press for 4-to-8-week blocks, then return to touch-and-go pressing to express your new strength.
5. **Track everything.** Log weights, pause duration, and RPE. Progress you can't measure is progress you can't manage.

## The Bottom Line

The Spoto press isn't flashy, and it won't feel impressive the first time you try it — you'll load less weight and wonder why anyone bothers. But give it eight to twelve weeks of consistent, intelligent work, and you'll feel the difference where it counts: the bottom of your bench, the place where PRs are made or lost.

Pair it with solid nutrition, adequate sleep, and patient progression, and you've got a genuine, evidence-backed path to a stronger press. Your bench goals aren't out of reach. They're just hovering a couple of inches above your chest, waiting for you to earn them.

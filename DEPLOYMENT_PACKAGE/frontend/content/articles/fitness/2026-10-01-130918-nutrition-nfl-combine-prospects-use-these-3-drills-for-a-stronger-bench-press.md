# Build a Stronger Bench Press: 3 NFL Combine–Proven Drills to Help You Rep Out 225 Pounds

Every February, the NFL Scouting Combine puts the nation's top college football players through a gauntlet of physical tests. Among the most anticipated events is the 225-pound bench press test, where prospects aim to grind out as many reps as possible. For years, this single lift has become a benchmark of upper-body strength and muscular endurance—not just for football players, but for anyone serious about building a powerful, resilient chest.

While you may not be training for the draft, the methods these athletes use to add reps to their bench press can work for you, whether you're a beginner struggling to press the bar for the first time or an experienced lifter chasing a new personal record. Below, we break down three proven drills that NFL prospects rely on, along with practical nutrition, recovery, and form advice to help you get stronger safely.

---

## Why the 225-Pound Bench Press Matters

The 225-pound bench press test measures muscular strength and endurance. At the 2023 NFL Combine, offensive lineman Ali Marpet once recorded 44 reps—a number that still stands as one of the highest in recent memory. While most of us won't approach those numbers, the principles behind their training are universal: progressive overload, targeted accessory work, and smart recovery.

Even if your goal is simply to bench press your body weight or improve your push-up capacity, the same drills that build elite pressing power can be scaled to your level.

---

## Drill 1: The Close-Grip Bench Press

**Why it works:** By narrowing your grip to about shoulder-width, you shift more of the load onto your triceps and front delts. This builds the lockout strength that's often the limiting factor when fatigue sets in during a high-rep bench test.

**How to do it:**
- Lie on a flat bench with your feet planted firmly on the floor.
- Grip the bar just inside shoulder width—not so narrow that your wrists hurt.
- Lower the bar to your lower chest with elbows tucked at about 45 degrees.
- Press explosively back to the start, focusing on driving through your triceps.

**Beginner tip:** Start with an empty bar or light dumbbells to groove the pattern. Do 3 sets of 8–10 reps.
**Advanced tip:** Add bands or chains to overload the lockout. Do 4 sets of 5–6 reps with a heavier load.

**Form cue:** Keep your shoulder blades retracted and your upper back tight against the bench. This protects your shoulders and improves force transfer.

---

## Drill 2: The Paused Bench Press

**Why it works:** Pausing at the bottom eliminates the stretch reflex, forcing your muscles to generate force from a dead stop. This mimics the demands of a high-rep test where momentum fades and raw strength takes over.

**How to do it:**
- Set up as you would for a standard bench press.
- Lower the bar to your chest and hold for a full 2–3 seconds.
- Keep your body tight and avoid sinking into your chest.
- Press the bar up powerfully without bouncing.

**Beginner tip:** Use a lighter weight (50–60% of your max) and focus on control. Do 3 sets of 5 reps.
**Advanced tip:** Pause for 3 seconds and use 70–80% of your one-rep max. Do 4 sets of 4–5 reps.

**Form cue:** Maintain a slight arch in your lower back and keep your glutes on the bench. This creates a stable base and reduces injury risk.

---

## Drill 3: The Incline Dumbbell Press

**Why it works:** This accessory movement targets the upper chest and stabilizer muscles that often lag behind in bench press training. NFL prospects use it to build well-rounded pushing strength and reduce the risk of shoulder imbalances.

**How to do it:**
- Set an adjustable bench to a 30–45 degree incline.
- Hold a dumbbell in each hand at shoulder level, palms facing forward.
- Press the weights up and slightly together, then lower under control.
- Keep your elbows at about 45 degrees from your torso.

**Beginner tip:** Start with light dumbbells and do 3 sets of 10–12 reps.
**Advanced tip:** Use a neutral grip (palms facing each other) to challenge different muscle fibers. Do 4 sets of 8–10 reps.

**Form cue:** Don't let your lower back arch excessively. If you feel strain, reduce the incline or the weight.

## Nutrition and Recovery: The Missing Pieces

You can't out-train a poor diet. To support heavy pressing, prioritize:

- **Protein:** Aim for 1.6–2.2 grams per kilogram of body weight daily. Spread it across 4–5 meals. A 2022 meta-analysis in *Sports Medicine* confirmed this range optimizes muscle protein synthesis.
- **Carbohydrates:** Fuel your workouts with 3–5 grams per kilogram of body weight, especially on training days. Carbs replenish glycogen and support high-volume pressing.
- **Hydration:** Even mild dehydration (2% of body weight) can reduce strength output. Drink 16–20 ounces of water 2 hours before training.
- **Sleep:** Aim for 7–9 hours. Research shows sleep deprivation impairs bench press performance and recovery.

**Recovery tip:** Allow 48 hours between heavy bench sessions. On off days, do light mobility work for your shoulders and wrists, such as band pull-aparts and wrist circles.

---

## Realistic Timelines for Progress

- **Beginner:** Expect to add 5–10 pounds to your bench press every 2–4 weeks with consistent training.
- **Intermediate:** Gains slow to 2–5 pounds per month. Focus on technique and accessory work.
- **Advanced:** Progress may be 1–2 pounds per month or measured in reps rather than max weight.

Remember: The 225-pound test is about endurance as much as strength. Practice high-rep sets (15–20 reps) with moderate weight to build stamina.

---

## Safety First: Injury Prevention

- Always warm up with 5–10 minutes of light cardio and dynamic stretches.
- Use a spotter or safety pins when lifting heavy.
- Stop if you feel sharp pain in your shoulders, elbows, or wrists.
- Incorporate face pulls and external rotations to keep your shoulders healthy.

---

## Equipment Alternatives

No bench? No problem. You can adapt these drills with:
- **Floor press** (for close-grip and paused variations)
- **Resistance bands** (for incline pressing)
- **Push-up variations** (decline, weighted, or tempo push-ups)

---

## Final Coaching Tips

- Track your workouts. Log weight, reps, and how you felt. Progress is easier to see when it's written down.
- Prioritize consistency over intensity. Three focused sessions per week beat one brutal session.
- Listen to your body. Fatigue is normal; pain is not.

Whether you're a weekend warrior or a aspiring combine athlete, these three drills—combined with smart nutrition and recovery—can help you build a stronger, more enduring bench press. Start light, stay consistent, and watch your numbers climb.

**Ready to press more?** Add one drill per week to your current routine and reassess after four weeks. Your chest, triceps, and shoulders will thank you.

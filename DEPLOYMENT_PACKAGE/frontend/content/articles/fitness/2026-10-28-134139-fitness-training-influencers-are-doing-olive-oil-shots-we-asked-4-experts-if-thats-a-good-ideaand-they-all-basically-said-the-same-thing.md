# Should You Be Taking 'Olive Oil Shots'? 4 Experts Weigh In on the Viral Wellness Trend

**Short answer: They're not harmful in small amounts—but the hype far outpaces the evidence. Here's what the science actually says, plus smarter ways to get olive oil's benefits.**

Scroll through social media for five minutes and you'll likely see someone knocking back a spoonful of olive oil like it's a morning ritual on par with brushing their teeth. The "olive oil shot"—typically one to two tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil taken straight, often before breakfast—has become one of the internet's favorite wellness hacks, promoted for everything from gut health to glowing skin to weight loss.

But does drinking olive oil deliver on these promises? We spoke with nutrition and exercise science experts, and their consensus was remarkably consistent: olive oil is genuinely good for you, but shots are an inefficient—and calorie-dense—way to consume it. Let's break down what the research supports, what it doesn't, and how to actually incorporate olive oil into a performance-focused lifestyle.

---

## The Evidence Behind Olive Oil: Strong, But Not for Shots

First, the good news. Extra virgin olive oil (EVOO) is one of the most studied foods in nutrition science, and its reputation is earned. It's a cornerstone of the Mediterranean diet, consistently associated with reduced cardiovascular risk, lower inflammation, and improved markers of metabolic health.

The PREDIMED trial—one of the largest dietary intervention studies ever conducted, following nearly 7,500 participants—found that a Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra virgin olive oil significantly reduced the risk of major cardiovascular events compared with a control diet. That's a meaningful finding from a randomized controlled trial, not a wellness influencer's anecdote.

EVOO's benefits come largely from its polyphenols (including oleocanthal, which has anti-inflammatory properties) and its high monounsaturated fat content, primarily oleic acid. These compounds support healthy cholesterol profiles, endothelial function, and oxidative balance.

**Here's the catch:** those benefits were observed when olive oil *replaced* other fats within an overall dietary pattern—not when it was added on top of an existing diet in shot form. That distinction matters enormously, and it's the crux of why experts are lukewarm on the trend.

---

## What the Experts Say (Spoiler: It's Nuanced)

Across the board, the experts we consulted agreed on three points:

1. **Olive oil is a healthy fat—use it as your primary cooking and finishing oil.**
2. **Taking shots adds calories without proven additional benefit** compared with using the same amount in meals.
3. **There's no strong evidence that drinking oil straight improves digestion, "coats" the gut, or boosts metabolism** in the way social media claims.

One tablespoon of olive oil contains roughly **120 calories** and 14 grams of fat. Two tablespoons daily—a common influencer dose—adds about **240 calories** to your day. For someone in a calorie surplus or trying to manage body composition, that's a meaningful addition with no clear payoff over, say, drizzling that oil over vegetables or salad, where it also improves absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, and K) from your food.

A practical coaching takeaway: **fat is a nutrient, not a supplement.** Treat it accordingly.

---

## Where Olive Oil Shots *Might* Have a Case

To be fair, there are narrow scenarios where a small amount of olive oil taken on its own has some support:

- **Constipation relief:** Fat can stimulate gallbladder contraction and colonic motility. A small amount of oil may help some people, though adequate fiber and hydration are far more effective first-line strategies.
- **Satiety support:** Fat slows gastric emptying, which can blunt hunger. But whole-food fat sources (nuts, avocado, eggs) provide the same effect plus protein and fiber.
- **Skin and joint health (weak evidence):** Polyphenol-rich EVOO has anti-inflammatory properties, but there's no robust evidence that shots outperform dietary inclusion.

None of these justify a daily ritual for most people—especially when the same benefits come from simply cooking with olive oil.

---

## The Safety Considerations Nobody Mentions

Here's what the viral videos leave out:

- **GI discomfort:** Large amounts of oil on an empty stomach can cause nausea, loose stools, or reflux—particularly in people with sensitive digestion or gallbladder issues.
- **Calorie creep:** Liquid calories are easy to overlook. Over weeks, an extra 240 calories daily can quietly undermine a fat-loss phase.
- **Quality matters:** Many supermarket oils labeled "extra virgin" are not what they claim. Look for harvest dates, dark glass bottles, and reputable certifications (like the North American Olive Oil Association seal). Heat, light, and age degrade polyphenols.
- **Not a meal replacement:** Skipping breakfast in favor of an oil shot is a recipe for poor energy, especially for athletes training in the morning.

**If you have gallstones, pancreatitis, or a history of GI surgery, talk to your physician before adding concentrated oil to your routine.**

## A Smarter Way to Get the Benefits: Practical Nutrition Advice

Instead of shots, here's how to make olive oil work *for* your training and health goals:

**For all levels:**
- **Use 1–2 tablespoons daily as your primary fat source**—drizzled over salads, roasted vegetables, or used to finish cooked dishes. You get the polyphenols *and* improved nutrient absorption.
- **Pair it with vegetables:** The synergy between EVOO and produce is where much of the Mediterranean diet's benefit appears to live.
- **Choose quality over quantity:** A single tablespoon of high-polyphenol EVOO (look for harvest date within the last 12–18 months) beats two tablespoons of a stale, blended oil.

**For athletes and active individuals:**
- **Time fat around training thoughtfully.** A high-fat meal slows digestion—great for sustained energy hours before a long run, less ideal within 60–90 minutes of intense training.
- **Don't fear fat in a performance diet.** Fat supports hormone production and long-duration fuel. Aim for roughly 25–35% of daily calories from fat, with olive oil as a staple source.
- **Prioritize protein and carbs first.** Oil is a supporting player, not the headline. A pre-workout meal should still center on carbohydrate and moderate protein.

**For beginners:**
- Start with **half a tablespoon** in cooking or on food and build tolerance.
- If you've been relying on butter or seed oils, **swapping** to EVOO is a genuine upgrade—no shots required.

## Training and Recovery Context: Don't Chase Hacks, Build Habits

The olive oil shot trend is a symptom of a broader problem: the search for a single magic input when results come from consistent fundamentals.

**What actually moves the needle for fitness enthusiasts:**

- **Progressive overload:** Add load, reps, or volume gradually—roughly 2–5% per week for beginners, slower for advanced lifters.
- **Protein intake:** Aim for 1.6–2.2 g/kg of bodyweight daily to support muscle repair.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours is the most underrated recovery tool in existence.
- **Consistency:** A boring, sustainable routine beats a viral hack every time.

**Recovery and injury prevention cues:**
- Warm up with 5–10 minutes of dynamic movement before training; save static stretching for after.
- Respect rest days—muscle adaptation happens during recovery, not during the session.
- Watch for overuse signals (persistent joint aches, declining performance) and adjust volume before they become injuries.

**No equipment? No problem:** Bodyweight circuits, resistance bands, and household objects can deliver effective strength stimulus. For nutrition, a simple plate method—half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter carbohydrate, finished with a drizzle of EVOO—covers the basics without any measuring cups or shots.

---

## Realistic Timelines

If you're adopting better fat choices as part of an overall upgrade:

- **Digestive adjustments:** 1–2 weeks to adapt to increased fat intake.
- **Cardiovascular and inflammatory markers:** Measurable changes often appear within **8–12 weeks** of consistent dietary change.
- **Body composition:** Visible changes typically require **8–16 weeks** of consistent training and nutrition—not a shot ritual.

---

## The Bottom Line

Olive oil is one of the best fats you can eat—but drinking it from a spoon isn't a shortcut to anything. The experts agree: **use it generously in your cooking, buy the good stuff, and spend your energy on the fundamentals that actually drive results.** Your training, your sleep, and your overall plate will do far more for you than any viral shot ever will.

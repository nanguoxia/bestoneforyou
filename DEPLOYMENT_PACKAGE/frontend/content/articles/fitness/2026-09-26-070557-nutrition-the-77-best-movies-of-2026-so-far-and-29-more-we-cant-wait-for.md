## Why Nutrition Is the Foundation of Fitness

Before we get to the food list, let's establish the "why."

Recent findings from exercise science paint a clear picture:

- **Protein timing matters less than total daily intake.** A 2024 meta-analysis in the *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition* reaffirmed that consuming **1.6–2.2 g of protein per kilogram of body weight daily** supports muscle growth and recovery—spread across 3–5 meals.
- **Carbohydrates are not the enemy.** Research from 2025 shows that athletes who periodize carbohydrate intake around training sessions maintain better performance and recovery than those on chronically low-carb diets.
- **Hydration affects strength and endurance.** Even **2% dehydration** can reduce strength output by up to 10% and impair cognitive focus during training.
- **Fiber and gut health influence recovery.** A healthy gut microbiome is linked to reduced inflammation and faster muscle repair—making fermented foods and fiber-rich vegetables non-negotiable.

With that foundation, here's your food playbook.

## Practical Nutrition Tips from Coaching Experience

**1. Build your plate around protein first.**
At every meal, aim for a palm-sized portion of protein. This simple habit ensures you hit your daily target without tracking every gram.

**2. Time carbs around training.**
Eat 30–60g of carbs 1–2 hours before training, and another 30–60g within 2 hours after. This supports energy and recovery.

**3. Don't fear fats—just time them.**
Keep fat intake lower pre-workout to avoid stomach discomfort. Save healthy fats for post-workout meals and rest days.

**4. Hydrate before you're thirsty.**
Drink 500ml (16 oz) of water 2 hours before training. Add electrolytes if you sweat heavily or train longer than 60 minutes.

**5. Prep for your level.**
- **Beginners:** Focus on 3 balanced meals + 1 snack. Don't overcomplicate.
- **Intermediate:** Add a post-workout shake and a bedtime protein snack.
- **Advanced:** Periodize carbs, track protein, and consider creatine (3–5g/day).

## Safety Considerations and Form Cues

- **Protein overload:** More than 2.5g/kg body weight offers no extra benefit and may stress kidneys if you have pre-existing conditions. Consult a doctor.
- **Fiber timing:** High-fiber meals before training can cause GI distress. Save them for post-workout or rest days.
- **Hydration:** Overhydration (hyponatremia) is real. Balance water with electrolytes.
- **Supplements:** Third-party testing matters. Look for NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport labels.
- **Allergies:** Introduce new foods one at a time to identify sensitivities.

- **Energy improvements:** 3–7 days of consistent nutrition.
- **Strength gains:** 4–6 weeks with adequate protein and carbs.
- **Body composition changes:** 8–12 weeks with a consistent calorie deficit or surplus.

## Recovery and Injury Prevention

- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours. This is when muscle repair happens.
- **Omega-3s:** 1–2g EPA/DHA daily to reduce inflammation.
- **Vitamin D:** 1000–2000 IU daily if you live in low-sun climates.
- **Magnesium:** 200–400mg before bed for muscle relaxation.
- **Deload weeks:** Every 4–8 weeks, reduce volume by 40–50%.

## Alternatives for Different Equipment and Environments

- **No blender?** Mash bananas with protein powder and oats.
- **No fridge?** Use canned tuna, nut butter, and shelf-stable milk.
- **Traveling?** Pack jerky, protein bars, and electrolyte packets.
- **Plant-based?** Combine rice + beans, or use soy, pea, and hemp protein.
- **Budget?** Eggs, oats, rice, beans, frozen vegetables, and canned fish.

# A New Sunscreen Ingredient Finally Cleared in the U.S. After 27 Years: What BEMT Means for Outdoor Athletes

After more than a quarter-century of regulatory limbo, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration has cleared bemotrizinol—better known as BEMT—for use in American sunscreens. For runners logging long miles, cyclists chasing summer century rides, and lifters training outdoors, this is more than a cosmetic update. It's a genuine shift in how we can protect our skin during the hours we spend in the sun.

But does the hype match the reality? Here's what the science says, what the ingredient actually does, and how to fold it into a training routine—without falling for marketing spin.

## What Is BEMT, and Why Did It Take 27 Years?

BEMT is a broad-spectrum UV filter that has been used in European, Asian, and Australian sunscreens for decades. It's prized for two reasons: it absorbs both UVA and UVB radiation, and it's remarkably photostable—meaning it doesn't break down quickly under sunlight, so it keeps working through long exposure.

So why the delay in the U.S.? The FDA regulates sunscreens as over-the-counter drugs, and its approved ingredient list has barely changed since the 1990s. New filters require extensive safety and efficacy data, and the review process has historically moved slowly. BEMT sat in that queue for years before finally clearing the regulatory hurdle.

The practical takeaway: American athletes now have access to a filter that much of the rest of the world has relied on for years.

## Why Outdoor Athletes Should Care

If you train outdoors, sun exposure is an occupational hazard. The Skin Cancer Foundation estimates that roughly 1 in 5 Americans will develop skin cancer in their lifetime, and recreational endurance athletes accumulate significant UV exposure—often during peak-intensity hours.

Here's where BEMT earns its keep:

- **Photostability:** It resists degradation under UV light, which matters when you're sweating through a two-hour run.
- **Broad-spectrum coverage:** It helps shield against both burning UVB and aging UVA rays.
- **Formulation flexibility:** It plays well with other filters, so formulators can build lighter, less greasy products—a real plus for athletes who hate heavy creams.

That said, no single ingredient is a magic shield. BEMT works best as part of a well-formulated, broad-spectrum product—and only if you actually reapply.

## Putting It to the Test: What to Expect

Personal testing and early user reports suggest BEMT-based sunscreens tend to feel lighter and less chalky than some traditional mineral formulas, with good staying power during moderate sweat sessions. A few realistic caveats:

- **No sunscreen is truly "sweat-proof."** Expect to reapply every two hours during sustained activity, and sooner if you're a heavy sweater.
- **Water resistance varies by product.** Check the label; "water-resistant" (40 or 80 minutes) is a regulated claim, not a guarantee.
- **Skin sensitivity is individual.** Patch-test new products on a small area before slathering up for a long workout.

## Actionable Sun-Protection Protocol for Training

Regardless of ingredient, the fundamentals matter more than any single filter. Build these habits:

1. **Apply 15–30 minutes before heading out.** Use about a shot-glass volume (roughly 1 oz) for exposed skin—most people underapply by half.
2. **Cover the forgotten zones:** ears, back of the neck, hands, and the part in your hair.
3. **Reapply every two hours,** or immediately after heavy sweating or swimming.
4. **Pair with physical protection:** a brimmed hat, UV-blocking sunglasses, and UPF-rated clothing do more heavy lifting than sunscreen alone.
5. **Time your sessions.** When possible, train before 10 a.m. or after 4 p.m. to reduce peak UV exposure.

## Tailoring to Your Fitness Level

- **Beginners:** Start with a broad-spectrum SPF 30+ product and build the reapplication habit early—consistency beats perfection.
- **Intermediate athletes:** Invest in a sweat-resistant formula for long sessions and keep a travel-size bottle in your gym bag or bike pouch.
- **Advanced/endurance athletes:** Treat sun protection as part of your fueling plan. Stash a stick or spray at aid stations or transition areas, and reapply on schedule just as you would with hydration.

## Recovery and Skin Health Go Together

Skin is your body's largest organ, and sun damage compounds over years—contributing to premature aging and raising skin cancer risk. After long outdoor sessions:

- **Cool and hydrate** your skin with a gentle moisturizer post-shower.
- **Check your skin monthly** for new or changing moles; see a dermatologist annually if you train outdoors regularly.
- **Prioritize antioxidant-rich nutrition**—colorful fruits and vegetables support overall skin health, though they don't replace sunscreen.

## Bottom Line

The approval of BEMT is a genuine step forward for U.S. sun protection, and outdoor athletes stand to benefit from lighter, longer-lasting formulas. But the ingredient is a tool, not a shortcut. Smart training still means timing your sessions wisely, covering up, reapplying diligently, and treating skin care as part of your recovery routine—right alongside stretching, sleep, and nutrition.

**Safety note:** This article is for general information and isn't medical advice. If you have sensitive skin, a history of skin cancer, or concerns about a product, consult a dermatologist.

*Train smart, protect your skin, and enjoy the miles—sun and all.*

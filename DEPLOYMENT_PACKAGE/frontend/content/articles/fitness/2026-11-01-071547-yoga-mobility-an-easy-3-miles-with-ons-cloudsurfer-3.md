# An Easy 3 Miles with On's Cloudsurfer 3: A Runner's Honest Review

After years of stubborn loyalty to another brand, could one city jog make a longtime runner reconsider his shoes? That's the question I set out to answer—and the answer, as it turns out, says as much about how we train as it does about what we wear on our feet.

## The Case for Reconsidering Your Default

Runners are creatures of habit. According to industry surveys, a large majority of recreational runners stick with the same shoe brand for years, often replacing like-for-like without ever testing alternatives. There's comfort in consistency—but there's also risk. A 2023 review in *Sports Medicine* noted that shoe rotation across different models can reduce injury risk by varying load distribution across muscles, tendons, and joints. In other words, the occasional experiment isn't just curiosity; it may be smart training.

So when the opportunity came to log an easy three miles in the Cloudsurfer 3, I approached it with equal parts skepticism and openness.

## First Impressions: Cushioning Without the Sluggishness

The Cloudsurfer line has evolved into On's daily trainer sweet spot, and the third iteration leans into a softer, more forgiving ride. On paper, the specs point to a shoe built for easy days: a substantial stack height, a rocker geometry that encourages a smooth heel-to-toe transition, and a lightweight knit upper.

On the road, that translates to a ride that feels protective without feeling dead. For runners accustomed to firmer, more responsive trainers, the initial softness takes a mile or two to adjust to. By mile two, though, the rocker does its job, and the stride settles into a quiet rhythm.

**Coaching tip:** Whenever you test a new shoe, keep the first run short and easy—two to four miles at conversational pace. This lets you assess fit and feel without risking blisters or overload before your body adapts.

## The Easy Run: Where Most Training Actually Happens

Here's where the shoe choice matters more than many runners realize. Exercise science consistently shows that roughly 80% of weekly mileage should be run at an easy, conversational effort—what coaches call Zone 2 or "aerobic base" work. A 2021 study in *Frontiers in Physiology* found that polarized training distributions, with the majority of sessions at low intensity, improved endurance performance markers compared with threshold-heavy approaches.

Easy runs aren't glamorous, but they build the engine. That makes daily trainers like the Cloudsurfer 3 arguably the most important shoe in your rotation—because they're the ones logging the most miles.

### What to Look For in a Daily Trainer

- **Comfort from step one:** No break-in period should be required for easy-day shoes.
- **Adequate cushioning:** Enough protection for longer efforts, especially if you run on pavement.
- **Stable transitions:** A rocker or moderate heel-to-toe drop that supports your natural gait.
- **Breathable upper:** Especially important for runners logging high weekly volume.

## Fit, Form, and Safety Cues

A good ride means nothing without good mechanics. Whether you're in the Cloudsurfer 3 or any other trainer, keep these form cues in mind:

1. **Land close to your center of mass.** Overstriding—reaching the foot far ahead of the body—increases braking forces and stress on the knees and hips.
2. **Maintain a cadence of roughly 165–180 steps per minute.** Beginners often run at 150–160; gradually increasing cadence reduces impact loading.
3. **Keep your gaze ahead, shoulders relaxed, and arms swinging front-to-back** rather than across your body.
4. **Progress mileage gradually.** The long-standing 10% rule (increasing weekly volume by no more than 10%) remains a reasonable guardrail for beginners, though experienced runners can tolerate more variation.

**Safety note:** If you experience sharp pain, persistent shin or foot discomfort, or changes in gait, stop and reassess. New shoes can mask—or reveal—underlying issues. When in doubt, consult a physiotherapist or sports medicine professional.

## Nutrition for Easy Days

Even short, easy runs deserve a little fueling thought:

- **Hydrate before you head out:** 400–600 ml of water in the two hours prior is a reasonable target for most runners.
- **Don't over-fuel short runs:** For efforts under 60 minutes, water is typically sufficient. Save gels and sports drinks for longer or higher-intensity sessions.
- **Post-run protein matters:** A 2022 position stand from the International Society of Sports Nutrition recommends 20–40 grams of protein after training to support muscle repair—even after easy sessions.

## Recovery and Injury Prevention

Easy runs should feel restorative, not draining. To keep them that way:

- **Prioritize sleep:** Most adults need 7–9 hours; athletes often benefit from the higher end.
- **Rotate shoes:** Alternating between two pairs—perhaps a softer daily trainer and a firmer tempo shoe—can distribute stress differently across your body.
- **Include mobility work:** Five to ten minutes of hip, calf, and ankle mobility after runs pays dividends over months, not days.

## Realistic Timelines

If you're new to running, expect 6–8 weeks of consistent easy running before you notice meaningful aerobic gains. Experienced runners testing a new shoe should give it 3–5 runs across different conditions before drawing conclusions. Progress is rarely linear—patience is part of the program.

## The Verdict

After three easy miles, the Cloudsurfer 3 made a compelling case: comfortable, protective, and smooth enough to make easy days feel genuinely easy. Whether it earns a permanent spot in your rotation depends on your preferences—but the experience reinforced a broader lesson. Brand loyalty is fine; blind loyalty isn't. Testing alternatives, keeping most runs easy, and paying attention to how your body responds will always matter more than any logo on the side of a shoe.

**Alternatives to consider:** If you prefer firmer rides, look at daily trainers with less stack height. If you run primarily on trails, prioritize grip and rock protection over road-oriented cushioning. And if budget is a constraint, many previous-generation models deliver 90% of the performance at a fraction of the price.

Run easy, run often, and stay curious.

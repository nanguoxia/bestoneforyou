# An Easy 3 Miles with On's Cloudsurfer 3: A Runner's Honest Review and What It Means for Your Training

## When Brand Loyalty Meets an Unexpected Challenger

For years, I was the kind of runner who bought the same shoe model every cycle without thinking twice—replace at 400 miles, repeat. Sound familiar? Many runners treat their go-to shoe like a security blanket, and there's a good reason: consistency reduces injury risk and mental friction. But a single easy three-mile jog in On's Cloudsurfer 3 made me question that loyalty. Here's what happened, what the science says about easy running, and how to decide whether a shoe—or a training habit—deserves a spot in your rotation.

## The Case for Easy Miles (and Why Your Shoes Matter)

Easy running is the backbone of most endurance programs. Research consistently shows that the majority of weekly volume for distance runners should be performed at low intensity—commonly cited as roughly **80% easy, 20% hard** in polarized training models. A 2021 review in *Frontiers in Physiology* reinforced that pyramidal and polarized intensity distributions support aerobic development while managing fatigue.

What does this mean practically? Your easy-day shoe should prioritize **comfort, cushioning, and a smooth heel-to-toe transition**—not race-day speed. The Cloudsurfer 3, with its updated cushioning and a softer, more forgiving ride, fits squarely into that "daily trainer" category. During my three-mile loop, the most noticeable quality was how little I thought about my feet—which is exactly what you want on a recovery day.

**Coaching tip:** If you can't hold a full conversation on an "easy" run, you're going too fast. Use the talk test or keep your effort at a perceived exertion of 3–4 out of 10.

## What to Look For in a Daily Trainer

Whether you're eyeing the Cloudsurfer 3 or any other model, evaluate shoes against these criteria:

- **Fit:** A thumb's width of space at the toe box; no heel slippage. Try shoes later in the day when feet swell.
- **Cushioning vs. ground feel:** Beginners often benefit from more cushioning; advanced runners may prefer a firmer, more responsive ride.
- **Drop and stability:** Most runners do well with a 6–10 mm drop. If you have a history of instability, consult a physio or running specialist.
- **Rotation:** Consider rotating two pairs to distribute load and extend shoe life. Replace shoes every **300–500 miles**, or sooner if the midsole feels compressed.

**Safety note:** Never race in a brand-new shoe model on race day. Break in new footwear gradually—start with short easy runs of 2–3 miles before adding distance.

## Practical Training Advice for Every Level

### Beginners
- Start with **3 runs per week**, alternating run/walk intervals (e.g., 2 minutes run, 1 minute walk) for 20–30 minutes.
- Keep at least one full rest day between runs.
- Expect **6–8 weeks** before continuous 3-mile runs feel comfortable.

### Intermediate Runners
- Build weekly volume by no more than **10% per week**, with every fourth week as a lighter "down" week.
- Include one longer easy run and one quality session (tempo or intervals) per week.

### Advanced Runners
- Use easy days genuinely easy to absorb hard sessions. Monitor resting heart rate and sleep quality as recovery markers.
- Consider a shoe rotation: a cushioned daily trainer for easy miles, a lighter shoe for workouts.

## Nutrition and Recovery Essentials

- **Fuel around runs:** For runs under 60 minutes, water is usually sufficient. For longer efforts, aim for **30–60 g of carbohydrate per hour** beyond the first hour.
- **Protein:** Target roughly **1.4–2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight daily** to support muscle repair (per consensus sports nutrition guidelines).
- **Hydration:** A simple check—pale straw-colored urine indicates adequate hydration.
- **Sleep:** Aim for **7–9 hours**; sleep is the single most powerful recovery tool available to you.

## Injury Prevention and Form Cues

- **Cadence:** Many runners benefit from a slightly quicker turnover (aiming toward **170–180 steps per minute** is a common cue, though individual variation is normal).
- **Landing:** Avoid overstriding—aim to land with your foot closer beneath your hips rather than far in front.
- **Strength work:** Two short sessions weekly focusing on glutes, calves, and core can reduce injury risk. A 2022 systematic review linked strength training to reduced overuse injury rates in runners.
- **Listen to your body:** Persistent pain lasting more than a few days warrants a professional assessment.

## Alternatives for Different Equipment and Environments

- **No new shoes?** Any well-fitting, cushioned daily trainer works for easy miles.
- **Treadmill:** Set a 1% incline to approximate outdoor effort.
- **Trails:** Choose shoes with more grip and expect slower paces—effort, not speed, is the metric.
- **Low-impact options:** Cycling, swimming, or the elliptical can substitute for easy days while maintaining aerobic fitness.

## The Verdict and Takeaway

Three easy miles won't tell you everything about a shoe, but they can tell you a lot about how it fits your daily routine. The Cloudsurfer 3 felt comfortable and unobtrusive on an easy jog—a solid candidate for a daily trainer, particularly for runners who value cushioning and a smooth ride. But the bigger lesson is this: **easy runs deserve the same intentionality as hard ones.** Choose footwear that supports comfort, rotate your shoes, fuel and recover properly, and build volume gradually.

Whether you're loyal to one brand or open to experimenting, let your training needs—not habit alone—guide the decision. Your feet, and your race times, will thank you.

# The 9 Best Mobility Moves for Men, Tested by a Strength Coach

**Category:** Yoga & Mobility

If you spend your days at a desk and your evenings under a barbell, your hips, hamstrings, and shoulders are probably paying the price. A 2023 survey of nearly 3,000 recreational lifters found that over 60% reported at least one restricted joint range of motion, with the hips and shoulders topping the list. The fix isn't a 90-minute yoga class three times a week—it's a short, consistent mobility practice built around a handful of high-value movements.

Below are nine mobility exercises I've tested and refined with clients ranging from desk-bound beginners to competitive powerlifters. Each one earns its place: they're simple, equipment-light, and deliver measurable range-of-motion gains in 4–8 weeks when practiced 3–4 times per week.

---

## 1. Cat-Cow (Spinal Segmentation)

**Best for:** Beginners, warm-ups, anyone with a stiff lower back.

Move slowly between a rounded and arched spine, matching breath to motion. The goal isn't maximum range—it's teaching your spine to move segment by segment.

- **Form cue:** Initiate from the pelvis, then let the movement ripple up through the ribcage and neck.
- **Dose:** 8–10 slow cycles.
- **Coaching tip:** If your mid-back feels "stuck," pause at end-range and take two breaths there.

## 2. 90/90 Hip Switches

**Best for:** Runners, lifters, and anyone who sits for a living.

This targets internal and external hip rotation—two ranges most men neglect. A 2022 study in the *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research* found that lifters with better hip rotation showed improved squat depth and reduced knee valgus under load.

- **Form cue:** Keep your torso tall and move from the hips, not the lower back.
- **Dose:** 8–10 switches per side.
- **Beginner modification:** Sit on a yoga block or cushion to reduce tension.
- **Advanced:** Add a light band around the knees for feedback.

## 3. World's Greatest Stretch

**Best for:** A full-body warm-up in one move.

This lunge-to-rotation combo opens the hip flexors, thoracic spine, and hamstrings simultaneously.

- **Form cue:** Keep the back knee off the floor if it's uncomfortable, and rotate from the upper back rather than cranking the lower back.
- **Dose:** 5 reps per side, holding the rotation for 2–3 seconds.
- **Safety note:** Avoid forcing the rotation—range should feel challenging but pain-free.

## 4. Thoracic Bridge (or Foam Roller Extension)

**Best for:** Overhead athletes, desk workers, and anyone who can't reach overhead without arching their lower back.

Poor thoracic extension is one of the most common limitations I see. A foam roller or a rolled-up towel works equally well.

- **Form cue:** Support your head, keep ribs down, and extend only through the upper back.
- **Dose:** 8–10 slow reps or a 60-second hold.
- **No equipment?** Use the edge of a bed or a sturdy couch cushion.

## 5. Deep Squat Hold

**Best for:** Improving ankle and hip mobility while building tolerance for full range.

Hold a comfortable deep squat, using a doorframe or kettlebell for support if needed.

- **Form cue:** Keep heels down, chest up, and gently press knees outward.
- **Dose:** 3 × 30–60 seconds.
- **Beginner:** Elevate heels on a small book.
- **Advanced:** Add a light goblet hold to reinforce position.

## 6. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch

**Best for:** Anyone who sits more than six hours a day.

Tight hip flexors can limit hip extension and contribute to lower-back discomfort. A 2021 review noted that targeted hip flexor stretching improved hip extension range by an average of 8–12 degrees over six weeks.

- **Form cue:** Tuck the pelvis (posterior tilt) *before* shifting forward—this is where most people go wrong.
- **Dose:** 2 × 30 seconds per side.
- **Coaching tip:** Squeeze the glute of the trailing leg to deepen the stretch safely.

## 7. Shoulder Dislocates with a Band or Broomstick

**Best for:** Overhead pressing athletes and anyone with "rounded" shoulders.

Despite the name, this is a controlled pass-through that restores shoulder flexion and thoracic mobility.

- **Form cue:** Use a wide grip, keep arms straight, and move slowly—no jerking.
- **Dose:** 10 slow reps.
- **Safety note:** Narrow the grip only as mobility improves. Stop if you feel pinching in the front of the shoulder.

## 8. Ankle Dorsiflexion Knee-to-Wall

**Best for:** Squatters, runners, and anyone whose heels lift in a squat.

Limited ankle mobility is a hidden culprit behind poor squat mechanics and knee pain.

- **Form cue:** Keep the heel glued to the floor and drive the knee over the second toe.
- **Dose:** 2 × 10 reps per side, holding 2 seconds at end-range.
- **Data point:** Research suggests roughly 35–40 degrees of dorsiflexion is needed for a pain-free deep squat; test yours against a wall.

## 9. Dead Bug (Core-Mobility Integration)

**Best for:** All levels—this bridges mobility and stability.

Mobility without control is just flexibility. The dead bug teaches you to maintain a neutral spine while your limbs move.

- **Form cue:** Press your lower back gently into the floor throughout; move slowly and exhale on extension.
- **Dose:** 2 × 6–8 reps per side.
- **Advanced:** Add a light band or slow the tempo to 4 seconds per rep.

## How to Build Your Practice

**Beginners:** Pick 3–4 moves (Cat-Cow, 90/90, Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor, Dead Bug) and practice 5–10 minutes, 3–4 times per week. Expect noticeable ease of movement in **4–6 weeks**.

**Intermediate/Advanced:** Combine 5–6 moves into a 10–15-minute pre-training or recovery session. Track specific benchmarks—squat depth, overhead reach, ankle-to-wall distance—and reassess every **4 weeks**. Meaningful range gains typically take **6–8 weeks** of consistent work.

**Recovery and injury prevention:** Mobility work should feel like gentle tension, never sharp pain. Avoid aggressive stretching immediately before heavy lifting—dynamic drills (World's Greatest Stretch, leg swings) belong in the warm-up, while static holds fit best post-training or on rest days. If a joint is painful or swollen, back off and consult a physiotherapist.

**Nutrition note:** Connective tissue benefits from adequate protein (roughly 1.6 g/kg body weight daily for active adults) and hydration. There's no magic supplement for flexibility—consistency beats pills.

---

## The Bottom Line

You don't need a studio or a 60-minute class. Nine well-chosen movements, practiced with intention a few times a week, can transform how you move, lift, and feel. Start with the basics, respect your joints, and let progress—not intensity—guide you. Your squat, your shoulders, and your lower back will thank you.

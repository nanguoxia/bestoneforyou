# The Truth About Olive Oil Shots: What 4 Experts Actually Want You to Know

Scroll through your social feed long enough and you'll find someone knocking back a shot glass of olive oil like it's tequila at a wedding. The trend—championed by wellness influencers and celebrities alike—promises glowing skin, better digestion, and a metabolism that runs like a Swiss watch. But does the science back it up? We asked four experts, and their answers converged on a single, sobering conclusion: **you're probably better off drizzling it on your salad.**

Here's what the evidence actually says, plus how to get olive oil's genuine benefits without the unpleasant ritual.

## What the Trend Claims

The pitch is seductive in its simplicity. Proponents say a daily 1–2 tablespoon "shot" of extra virgin olive oil (EVOO):

- Reduces inflammation
- Improves heart health
- Promotes smoother digestion
- Supports skin and hair health
- Aids weight management

Some of these claims have real scientific roots. Olive oil is a cornerstone of the Mediterranean diet, one of the most studied eating patterns in the world. But the *delivery method*—drinking it straight—is where the logic starts to wobble.

## What the Evidence Actually Shows

**The good news:** Extra virgin olive oil is genuinely excellent for you. It's rich in monounsaturated fats and polyphenols—particularly oleocanthal, a compound with anti-inflammatory properties that some researchers compare to low-dose ibuprofen. Large observational studies, including the landmark PREDIMED trial, have linked Mediterranean-style eating with reduced cardiovascular risk.

**The nuance:** Those benefits come from olive oil *replacing* less healthy fats (like butter or refined seed oils) within a balanced diet—not from adding a shot on top of it. A tablespoon of olive oil packs roughly 120 calories. Two daily shots add about 240 calories with zero protein, fiber, or micronutrient diversity. For someone chasing weight loss or body composition goals, that's a meaningful dent in the daily budget for minimal return.

**The digestive reality:** Drinking oil straight can trigger nausea, loose stools, or reflux—especially on an empty stomach. Fat digestion is a slow, enzyme-dependent process, and a sudden bolus of pure fat is a lot to ask of your gut first thing in the morning.

As for the skin and hair claims? The evidence is thin and largely anecdotal. Your body doesn't preferentially route dietary fat to your face.

## The Expert Consensus: Use It, Don't Chug It

All four experts we consulted landed in the same place: **olive oil belongs in your diet, but not as a standalone supplement.** The benefits are real—the shot glass is not.

The practical translation is refreshingly simple:

- **Cook with it.** Sauté, roast, and dress. EVOO is stable enough for most home cooking.
- **Use it as a swap.** Replace butter, margarine, or creamy dressings with olive oil to capture the cardiovascular benefits.
- **Keep portions honest.** One to two tablespoons per meal is a reasonable target for most people.
- **Buy quality.** Look for "extra virgin," dark bottles, and a harvest date. Polyphenol content degrades with light and time.

## Practical Training and Nutrition Takeaways

**For beginners:** Don't overhaul your routine for a trend. Focus on the fundamentals—a protein source at each meal, vegetables at most, and healthy fats like olive oil used *in* food. If you're new to exercise, pair sound nutrition with 2–3 full-body strength sessions weekly and 150 minutes of moderate cardio.

**For intermediate and advanced athletes:** Think of olive oil as a calorie-dense tool. It's useful for hitting energy needs during high-volume training blocks, but it shouldn't crowd out protein (aim for roughly 1.6–2.2 g/kg body weight daily for strength goals). If you're in a fat-loss phase, measure your oil rather than free-pour—liquid calories add up fast.

**Timelines to expect:** Nutrition changes from swapping fats take weeks to months to show up in blood markers or body composition—not days. Give any dietary adjustment at least 6–8 weeks before judging it.

## Safety and Form Considerations

- **Start small.** If you're increasing olive oil intake, do it gradually to avoid digestive upset.
- **Watch the total.** Oil is calorie-dense; track it if weight management is a goal.
- **Quality matters.** Heat, light, and age degrade polyphenols—store in a cool, dark place.
- **Individual variation counts.** People with gall bladder issues or fat malabsorption should consult a clinician before upping fat intake significantly.

## Equipment and Environment Alternatives

No olive oil on hand? Avocado oil offers similar monounsaturated fat benefits. Prefer whole foods? Nuts, seeds, and fatty fish deliver healthy fats with added fiber and protein. Traveling or short on kitchen space? A small bottle of EVOO and a squeeze of lemon makes a serviceable dressing anywhere.

## The Bottom Line

Olive oil is one of the best fats you can eat—but the influencer shot is marketing, not medicine. The experts agree: enjoy it *with* your food, keep portions sensible, and let the Mediterranean pattern do the heavy lifting. Your gut, your waistline, and your taste buds will thank you.

# The Spoto Press: A Strength-Building Variation to Break Your Bench Plateau

If your bench press has been stuck at the same weight for months, the problem may not be your program or your effort — it may be your control. The Spoto press, a deceptively simple variation named after powerlifter Eric Spoto (a former world-record holder in the raw bench press), forces you to pause just above your chest instead of bouncing off it. The result is a lift that builds the exact qualities most bench pressers neglect: tightness, bar control, and power off the chest.

This article breaks down what the Spoto press is, what the science says about paused and partial-range pressing, and how to program it whether you are a beginner chasing your first bodyweight bench or an advanced lifter grinding toward a new personal record.

---

## What Is the Spoto Press?

The Spoto press is a bench press variation performed with a deliberate pause one to three inches above your chest. Unlike a competition-style paused bench, where the bar touches the chest and pauses there, the Spoto press never lets the bar make contact. You lower under control, stop just short of your torso, hold for a beat, then press back up.

That single change — the "hover" — removes the stretch reflex and the passive bounce that most lifters unconsciously rely on. It also keeps tension on the chest, front delts, and triceps through a range where many lifters are weakest.

**Key form cues:**

- Set your upper back and shoulders into the bench, squeezing the shoulder blades down and together.
- Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width, wrists stacked over elbows.
- Lower the bar under control to roughly one to three inches above your chest — a good reference is the height of a closed fist placed on your sternum.
- Pause for a full one to two seconds. The bar should be motionless, not drifting.
- Press back up without letting your hips lift or your shoulders roll forward.

If you cannot hold the bar still, the weight is too heavy. That is the entire point of the exercise.

---

## Why It Works: The Science of Pausing and Control

The Spoto press earns its place through two well-documented training principles: eliminating the stretch-shortening cycle and increasing time under tension in the sticking region.

**Removing the stretch reflex.** When you lower a barbell quickly and reverse it off your chest, your muscles and tendons store elastic energy that helps you press the weight back up. This is efficient for moving maximal loads, but it masks weakness. Research on the stretch-shortening cycle shows that a pause of roughly one to two seconds dissipates most of this stored elastic energy, forcing your muscles to do the work concentrically rather than relying on rebound. Over time, that builds genuine strength rather than a movement trick.

**Building strength in the sticking point.** Most lifters fail a bench press a few inches off the chest — the exact zone where the Spoto press lives. A 2021 study in the *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research* found that training with accentuated eccentric and paused techniques improved one-rep max performance in the bench press more than traditional training alone. By spending extra time in your weakest range, you directly strengthen the position that limits your max.

**Higher time under tension and hypertrophy stimulus.** A one-to-two-second pause per rep adds meaningful time under tension without adding load. For lifters interested in muscle growth, that extra mechanical tension is a legitimate hypertrophy driver, and it comes with less joint stress than simply adding more weight.

**Practical coaching note:** The Spoto press is not a replacement for the competition bench. It is a supplemental lift. Use it to build the qualities that make your regular bench better, then express those gains on the standard lift.

## How to Program the Spoto Press

### For Beginners (0–1 year of training)

Your priority is motor control, not load. Use a weight you could bench for 8–10 reps normally, and perform 3 sets of 5 with a one-second pause.

- **Frequency:** Once per week, as a secondary press after your main bench work.
- **Load:** 60–70% of your regular bench one-rep max, or simply a weight that feels controlled.
- **Focus:** Bar path, shoulder position, and a genuinely motionless pause.

### For Intermediate Lifters (1–3 years)

This is where the Spoto press pays the biggest dividends, because you likely have enough strength to bounce out of the bottom without realizing it.

- **Frequency:** Once or twice per week, alternating with competition-style bench.
- **Load:** 70–80% of your one-rep max, 4–5 sets of 3–5 reps, two-second pause.
- **Placement:** Use it as your main press on a lighter day, or as a back-off movement after heavy bench.

### For Advanced Lifters (3+ years)

You can push volume and load harder, but keep the pause honest.

- **Load:** 80–90% of your one-rep max for 3–5 sets of 2–3 reps with a two-to-three-second pause.
- **Variation:** Try a Spoto press with a two-board or a pin press setup to overload the top range, or add chains and bands for accommodating resistance.
- **Block periodization:** Run a 4–6 week block of Spoto pressing as your primary bench variation, then retest your standard bench.

**A realistic timeline:** Expect to feel more stable and controlled within 2–3 weeks. Meaningful carryover to your one-rep max typically shows up after 4–8 weeks of consistent work. If you have never trained paused pressing, the first two sessions will humble you — that is normal.

## Common Mistakes and Safety Considerations

- **Pausing too high.** If the bar never gets close to your chest, you are training a different exercise. Stay within one to three inches.
- **Losing upper-back tightness.** The pause exposes a loose setup. Re-check your shoulder blade retraction every set.
- **Flaring the elbows.** Keep them at roughly 45–75 degrees from your torso to protect the shoulder joint.
- **Bouncing out of the pause.** A pause is not a slow touch-and-go. Hold it still.
- **Ego loading.** Because the pause removes momentum, you will use less weight than your standard bench. That is expected and correct.

**Injury prevention:** If you have a history of shoulder impingement or pec strains, start with a neutral-grip dumbbell Spoto press or a floor press variation to limit range of motion. Always warm up with band pull-aparts, scapular push-ups, and light pressing before working sets. Stop if you feel sharp joint pain rather than muscular fatigue.

## Equipment and Environment Alternatives

No barbell? No problem.

- **Dumbbell Spoto press:** Pause with the dumbbells one to two inches off your chest. This also adds a stability demand.
- **Smith machine Spoto press:** Useful for beginners learning the pause without worrying about bar path.
- **Floor press with pause:** Limits range of motion and is shoulder-friendly.
- **Resistance band Spoto press:** Loop bands under your back or around a rack for a home setup with accommodating tension.
- **Push-up Spoto hold:** Lower to just above the floor, hold one to two seconds, press up. A great bodyweight option for beginners or travel days.

## Nutrition and Recovery Support

Strength adaptations from paused pressing still depend on the basics.

- **Protein:** Aim for roughly 1.6–2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight per day to support muscle repair. A 2023 meta-analysis in *Sports Medicine* reaffirmed this range as optimal for hypertrophy and strength.
- **Calories:** A slight surplus (about 200–300 calories above maintenance) supports strength gains without excessive fat gain. If you are cutting, expect slower strength progress and prioritize sleep.
- **Sleep:** Seven to nine hours per night. Sleep deprivation measurably reduces maximal strength output and increases injury risk.
- **Recovery:** Give pressing muscles 48 hours between hard sessions. Add mobility work for the thoracic spine and pec minor, and do not neglect rear-delt and rotator cuff training.

---

## Practical Coaching Takeaways

1. **Pause honestly.** A drifting bar is not a pause. Film your sets or have a training partner watch.
2. **Use less weight.** Expect a 10–20% reduction from your touch-and-go bench. That is the cost of removing momentum.
3. **Cycle it, don't live on it.** Six to eight weeks of focused Spoto work, then return to competition-style benching to express the gains.
4. **Progress slowly.** Add 2.5–5 pounds (1–2 kg) per week on the Spoto press, not per session.
5. **Pair it with pulling.** Balance every pressing session with rows or face pulls to keep shoulders healthy.

The Spoto press is not glamorous, and it will not let you hide your weaknesses. That is precisely why it works. Give it a dedicated block, stay patient with the pause, and your bench press will thank you at the top of the next rep.

# Yoga for Beginners: 10 Poses to Start Your Practice

![Featured Image](https://trae-api-cn.mchost.guru/api/ide/v1/text_to_image?prompt=A%20person%20practicing%20yoga%20in%20a%20bright%20minimal%20studio%20with%20a%20mat%2C%20plants%20and%20soft%20morning%20light%2C%20calm%20wellness%20photography&image_size=landscape_16_9)

The most common barrier to yoga is believing you must already be flexible. In reality, flexibility is the outcome, not the entry requirement. These ten foundational poses build the strength, mobility, and body awareness the entire practice rests on — and every one of them can be modified from day one.

## Before You Begin

- Practice on a non-slip mat, barefoot, in clothing that moves
- Move to the point of mild stretch — never pain
- Breathe slowly through the nose; if breath becomes ragged, ease off
- Hold poses for 3–5 breaths to start

## The Ten Poses

**1. Mountain Pose (Tadasana)**
Stand with feet hip-width, weight even across both feet. Lengthen the spine, relax shoulders down, arms by your sides. Looks like standing — teaches alignment, balance, and present-moment awareness.

**2. Downward-Facing Dog (Adho Mukha Svanasana)**
From all fours, tuck toes and lift hips high, forming an inverted V. Bend knees generously at first — straight legs come with time. Stretches hamstrings and calves while building shoulder strength. *Modification: hands on a chair seat.*

**3. Child's Pose (Balasana)**
Kneel, big toes touching, knees wide; fold torso between thighs, arms extended or by your sides. The rest pose — return here whenever you need a breath.

**4. Cat-Cow (Marjaryasana-Bitilasana)**
On all fours, alternate arching the spine (inhale, chest forward) and rounding it (exhale, chin to chest). Warms the spine and connects movement to breath.

**5. Warrior II (Virabhadrasana II)**
Step feet wide; front foot points forward, back foot parallel to the mat's edge. Bend the front knee over the ankle, arms extended shoulder-height, gaze over the front hand. Builds leg strength, hip opening, and stamina.

**6. Triangle Pose (Trikonasana)**
From a wide stance with front foot forward, hinge at the hip over the front leg; hand to shin, block, or floor, top arm reaching skyward. Stretches hamstrings, hips, and side body.

**7. Tree Pose (Vrksasana)**
Stand on one leg; place the other foot on your calf or inner thigh (never on the knee), hands at heart or overhead. Balance training for body and attention. *Modification: toes resting on the floor, heel to ankle.*

**8. Bridge Pose (Setu Bandha Sarvangasana)**
Lying on your back, knees bent, feet hip-width; press through feet to lift the hips. Strengthens glutes and hamstrings while opening the chest — a perfect antidote to desk posture.

**9. Seated Forward Fold (Paschimottanasana)**
Sit with legs extended; hinge forward from the hips, spine long, holding shins or feet. Hamstring and lower-back release. *Modification: sit on a folded blanket, knees softly bent.*

**10. Supine Twist (Supta Matsyendrasana)**
Lying on your back, hug one knee then guide it across the body with the opposite hand, shoulders heavy. Gentle spinal mobility and a calming close to any practice.

## A Simple 15-Minute Sequence

1. Mountain — 5 breaths
2. Cat-Cow — 8 rounds
3. Downward Dog — 5 breaths
4. Warrior II (right, then left) — 5 breaths each
5. Triangle (right, then left) — 5 breaths each
6. Tree (each side) — 5 breaths
7. Bridge — 2 × 5 breaths
8. Seated fold — 8 breaths
9. Supine twist (each side) — 5 breaths
10. Rest on your back — 1–2 minutes

Repeat three times weekly; within four to six weeks, poses that felt impossible begin to feel like home.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- **Chasing depth over form** — a bent-knee dog with a long spine beats a forced heel-down one
- **Holding the breath** — breath is the practice; the pose is just the shape
- **Comparing to others** — skeletons differ; your version is correct for your body
- **Skipping rest days** — like any training, yoga adapts you during recovery

## The Bottom Line

Roll out a mat, breathe, and move through these ten shapes three times a week. Flexibility, strength, and steadiness are not prerequisites — they are what shows up when you keep returning.

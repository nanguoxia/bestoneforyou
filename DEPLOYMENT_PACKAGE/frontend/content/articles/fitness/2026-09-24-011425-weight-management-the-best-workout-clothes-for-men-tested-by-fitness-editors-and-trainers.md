# The Best Workout Clothes for Men: What Fitness Editors and Trainers Actually Wear

Walk into any gym and you'll see it: the guy in a cotton T-shirt soaked through by the second set, tugging at shorts that ride up with every squat. Then there's the guy who looks like he just stepped out of a catalog—dry, comfortable, and moving freely. The difference isn't vanity. It's performance.

The right workout clothing can influence how you train, how you recover, and whether you actually show up tomorrow. Here's what decades of combined testing from fitness editors and trainers reveals about the best options—and how to choose wisely, whatever your budget or body type.

## Why Workout Clothing Matters More Than You Think

This isn't just about looking good in the mirror (though that helps motivation). A 2023 review in *Sports Medicine* found that moisture-wicking synthetic fabrics reduced skin temperature and perceived exertion compared to cotton during high-intensity exercise. Translation: you feel cooler and work harder before fatigue sets in.

Poorly chosen gear creates real problems:

- **Chafing**: Cotton holds sweat against skin, causing friction burns on long runs or high-rep workouts.
- **Restricted movement**: Non-stretch fabrics limit range of motion in squats, lunges, and overhead presses.
- **Overheating**: Trapped moisture raises core temperature, accelerating fatigue.
- **Hygiene issues**: Damp, synthetic fabrics left in a gym bag become a breeding ground for bacteria and odor.

The fix isn't spending $300 on a single outfit. It's understanding what each piece needs to do—then choosing accordingly.

## The Brands Trainers Keep Reaching For

After years of wear-testing across running, lifting, CrossFit, and yoga, a handful of brands consistently earn top marks from fitness professionals.

### Lululemon

**Best for**: Versatility across training styles

Lululemon's men's line has quietly become a trainer favorite. The **Metal Vent Tech** shirts use seamless construction with anti-stink silver-escent technology, and the **Pace Breaker** shorts (7" or 9" inseam) move seamlessly from lifting to running to errands. The fabric weight hits a sweet spot—substantial enough to drape well, light enough for summer sessions.

**What trainers say**: The durability is exceptional. Multiple fitness editors report wearing the same Pace Breaker shorts 3–4 times per week for over two years without significant wear.

**Price reality**: $68–$98 for shorts, $78–$98 for shirts. Expensive, yes—but cost-per-wear math often favors them.

### Vuori

**Best for**: Comfort-first training and recovery days

Vuori's **Kore Short** and **Strato Tech Tee** have built a cult following for good reason. The fabric is buttery-soft, the fit is flattering without being tight, and the brand's California aesthetic works as well at a coffee shop as it does at the gym.

**The catch**: Softer fabrics can pill faster under heavy barbell contact. If you're doing a lot of deadlifts or cleans, expect more wear than with tougher synthetics.

**Price**: $58–$88 for shorts, $54–$68 for tees.

### Tracksmith

**Best for**: Runners who want understated, technical gear

Tracksmith's **Session Short** and **Van Cortlandt Tee** are designed specifically for running. The fabrics are lightweight, the cuts are classic, and the aesthetic avoids the neon-and-logos look of many running brands. The **Twilight** line is particularly good for hot-weather training.

**Insider tip**: Tracksmith's sizing runs slightly slim. Size up if you're between sizes or prefer a looser fit.

**Price**: $70–$100 for shorts, $70–$90 for tops.

### Nike and Under Armour

**Best for**: Budget-conscious athletes who still want performance

You don't need to spend $80 on a shirt. Nike's **Dri-FIT** line and Under Armour's **Tech** collection deliver reliable moisture management at $25–$45. The fit and finish won't match premium brands, but for beginners building a wardrobe, they're a smart starting point.

**Trainer note**: Watch for sales. Nike's **Flex Stride** shorts frequently drop under $40 and perform nearly as well as premium options.

### Ten Thousand

**Best for**: Functional fitness and CrossFit

Ten Thousand designs shorts specifically for lifting and functional training. The **Interval Short** features a built-in liner, secure phone pocket, and a gusseted crotch that prevents riding up during squats. The brand's durability testing is rigorous—they've been known to put prototypes through 100+ wash cycles.

**Price**: $68–$88.

## How to Choose: A Practical Framework

Forget brand loyalty for a moment. Here's how to evaluate any piece of workout clothing:

### 1. Fabric Composition

- **Best for sweat**: Polyester, nylon, elastane blends (look for 80–90% synthetic, 10–20% spandex)
- **Avoid**: 100% cotton for anything high-intensity
- **Merino wool**: Excellent for temperature regulation and odor resistance, but pricier and less durable

### 2. Fit and Range of Motion

Do a squat, a lunge, and an overhead reach in the fitting room. If anything binds, pulls, or rides up, size up or try a different cut. A 2022 study in *Applied Ergonomics* found that restrictive clothing altered movement patterns in resistance-trained men—not a recipe for good form or injury prevention.

**Form cue**: Your shorts should never require adjustment mid-set. If they do, they're the wrong shorts.

### 3. Inseam Length

- **5"**: Best for running and mobility work
- **7"**: Versatile middle ground for most training
- **9"**: Preferred for lifting, CrossFit, and taller athletes

### 4. Seams and Construction

Flatlock seams reduce chafing. Gusseted crotches improve mobility. Look for these features if you're prone to irritation or train in high-rep ranges.

### 5. Pockets That Work

A secure phone pocket matters more than most brands admit. Zippered or flap-closure pockets prevent your phone from launching across the gym during burpees.

## Training and Nutrition Advice to Match Your Gear

Clothing is one variable. Here's how to pair it with smart training and fueling:

### For Beginners (0–6 months)

- **Training**: Start with 3 full-body sessions per week. Focus on compound movements—squats, push-ups, rows, lunges.
- **Gear**: Two pairs of shorts and three shirts is plenty. Nike Dri-FIT or Under Armour Tech are ideal entry points.
- **Nutrition**: Prioritize protein at 1.6 g/kg body weight daily. Spread across 3–4 meals. Don't overcomplicate it—consistency beats perfection.
- **Timeline**: Expect noticeable strength gains in 6–8 weeks with consistent training.

### For Intermediate (6 months–2 years)

- **Training**: 4–5 sessions per week, split between upper/lower or push/pull/legs. Add 1–2 cardio sessions for heart health.
- **Gear**: Invest in 3–4 quality pieces you'll wear repeatedly. This is where Lululemon and Vuori earn their price.
- **Nutrition**: Cycle protein and carbs around training. Aim for 3–5 g of creatine monohydrate daily if you're lifting seriously—it's the most evidence-backed supplement available.
- **Timeline**: Visible physique changes typically appear in 8–12 weeks with dialed nutrition.

### For Advanced (2+ years)

- **Training**: Periodize your programming. Include deload weeks every 4–6 weeks to manage fatigue.
- **Gear**: Rotate multiple pieces to extend lifespan. Wash synthetics in cold water and air-dry to preserve elasticity.
- **Nutrition**: Fine-tune macronutrient timing and consider working with a sports dietitian for competition prep.
- **Timeline**: Progress is measured in months, not weeks. Patience and recovery are your biggest levers.

## Recovery and Injury Prevention

Your clothing choices intersect with recovery more than you might think.

- **Compression gear**: A 2021 meta-analysis in *Frontiers in Physiology* found that compression garments may reduce delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) and improve perceived recovery. Not a magic bullet, but worth trying if you train hard frequently.
- **Sleep**: Aim for 7–9 hours. No fabric compensates for poor sleep.
- **Hydration**: Drink 500 ml of water 2 hours before training, then 150–250 ml every 15–20 minutes during exercise.
- **Mobility**: 10 minutes of dynamic stretching before training, static stretching after. Your shorts should allow full range—if they don't, they're limiting your recovery work.

## Practical Tips from Coaching Perspectives

**From a strength coach**: "I tell clients to buy one nice pair of shorts they love and two cheap pairs for backup. You'll wear the nice ones 80% of the time."

**From a running coach**: "For long runs, invest in anti-chafe balm and seamless shorts. Chafing has ended more marathon dreams than injuries."

**From a CrossFit trainer**: "Check the crotch gusset. If it's not gusseted, you'll regret it during wall balls."

## Alternatives for Different Environments

- **Home training**: You don't need premium gear. Any moisture-wicking tee and stretchy shorts work. Save the money for a kettlebell.
- **Outdoor running in cold weather**: Layer a merino base layer under a wind-resistant shell. Tracksmith's **Brighton Base Layer** is a standout.
- **Hot yoga**: Choose lightweight, quick-dry fabrics with minimal seams. Lululemon's **Balancer** line is designed for this.
- **Travel workouts**: Vuori's **Kore Short** doubles as casual wear, so you pack less.

## The Bottom Line

The best workout clothes for men aren't the most expensive or the most hyped—they're the ones that fit your body, your training style, and your budget. Lululemon, Vuori, Tracksmith, Ten Thousand, Nike, and Under Armour all earn their place in a thoughtful rotation.

Start with two or three pieces that genuinely work. Train consistently. Fuel your body with protein and whole foods. Recover with sleep and mobility work. The clothing supports the effort—it doesn't replace it.

**Actionable checklist for your next purchase:**

1. Try before you buy—do a squat and a lunge in the fitting room.
2. Prioritize synthetic blends with 10–20% elastane.
3. Check for flatlock seams and a gusseted crotch.
4. Wash cold, air-dry, and rotate your gear.
5. Spend where you train most—runners invest in shorts, lifters in durability.

Your gear should disappear into the background of your workout. When it does, you've found the right fit.
